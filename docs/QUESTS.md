# Quest evidence and simplification policy

Real-case quests teach how to assess evidence. They distinguish public
transactions, outside records, inferences about people and authority to spend.
Privacy is useful for ordinary people; use of a privacy tool is not evidence of
criminal conduct by itself.

## Repeatable authoring and review

1. State one learning goal for each stage. Record what the observer knows before
   asking the question. Give each question one supported conclusion and explain
   why the other conclusions go beyond the evidence.
2. Use primary sources: court documents, official findings, admitted facts and
   dated agency announcements. Keep the source URL, date and relevant page or
   paragraph beside the claim. Check later pleas, judgments and corrections
   before publishing a historical accusation as an established outcome.
3. Attribute an investigator's affidavit to the investigator. Distinguish its
   evidence descriptions from later admissions and convictions. Name the actual
   offense of conviction; do not substitute a broader headline description.
4. Label reconstructed diagrams and invented labels clearly. Mark selected or
   compressed flows as such. A service's name or account holder is supplied
   context unless the player has evidence establishing it. Do not invent a
   complete public route across services from a partial official diagram.
5. Keep identity, technical control and legal entitlement distinct. Avoid claims
   that an address proves a person's identity, a private key proves rightful
   ownership, or tracing alone provides the ability to seize bitcoin.
6. Use historical dates for valuations and outcomes. A sentence imposed on a
   given date does not establish current custody. Do not claim that seized assets
   have been returned unless a source confirms the specific distribution.
7. Keep game data fictional or illustrative. Never request real wallet details,
   identity documents or keys. If numerical transactions are introduced, use
   integer satoshis and check inputs = outputs + fee. Keep both the interactive
   quest and its no-JavaScript lesson consistent with the sources.

Reviewers should be able to inspect every source from the relevant reveal and
the final case summary. A completion badge records finishing the lesson; it
does not certify a player's knowledge, financial privacy or legal status.

presenting every search warrant as a prior seizure warrant.

## Quest 03: The Fake Giveaway

Source review: **2026-10-18**. The starting event is the July 15, 2020 Twitter
account takeover and fake bitcoin giveaway. The game uses two invented account
labels, one non-payable address label and three illustrative incoming payments.
These are not screenshots, real addresses, exact transaction records or a
complete reconstruction of the investigation. The final receipt comparison is
an explicitly fictional privacy exercise.

The game only establishes that two invented posts share a destination; it does not infer endorsement or authorship from that link.                                                                                                            |

The three illustrated incoming payments are invented; the conclusion that transfers do not identify distinct people is the lesson's inference. No victim count is inferred.                                                      |
| Linking a public profile with payment data can add identity context                                       | [2020 investigator affidavit](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2020/07/31/download_fazeli_complaint_0.pdf), paragraphs 50–55, PDF pages 18–19                      | The affidavit describes combining publicly posted addresses with exchange records and other evidence. It is a probable-cause allegation, not a verdict. The game's public receipt is a separate invented example of linking context, not evidence claimed to have appeared in the real case. |
| O’Connor's later plea and sentence                                                                        | [DOJ sentencing announcement, 2023-06-23](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndca/pr/uk-citizen-sentenced-five-years-cybercrime-offenses), opening paragraph and charges paragraph            | May 9, 2023 plea; five-year sentence on June 23, 2023 for multiple cybercrime offenses including his role in the Twitter hack. Do not attribute every scam post to him or treat the sentence as solely for the giveaway. No claim about present custody.                                     |

The separate SIM-swap theft and its $794,012.64 forfeiture must not be described
as giveaway proceeds. The receipt comparison demonstrates what the act of
publishing adds: keeping it private does not erase an already-public transaction
or guarantee that other records cannot link it. A public profile link alone
does not prove control of every address in a wallet or identify a real person.

The **Signal Sleuth** completion badge is independent of the other rewards.
Players must inspect and answer all three clues and try the receipt comparison;
wrong guesses still earn the badge after the explanations. Progress remains in
tab memory. The reading edition contains the same lessons and comparison, while
interactive badge export requires JavaScript.
